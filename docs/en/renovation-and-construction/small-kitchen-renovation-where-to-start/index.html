Guide: Small Kitchen Renovation: Where to Start

Home Decor 07/09/2026 4 min read

Ultimate Guide: Small Kitchen Renovation – Where to Start Without Stress

Hello, kitchen renovator! If you're here, you've probably looked at your kitchen and thought: "I need a makeover, but where do I start?" Don't worry, you're not alone in this. Renovating a kitchen can seem challenging, but we'll show you that with a well-planned step-by-step approach, everything becomes simpler.

In this guide, you'll learn exactly where to start, what the essential steps are, and how to avoid the most common mistakes. The good news: you don't need to tear down walls or spend a fortune to transform your kitchen!

Let's use a culinary analogy: imagine your renovation is like preparing a special dish. First, you need the ingredients (planning), then follow the recipe in the correct order (execution), and finally finish with the seasonings (the details that make the difference).

And if at any point you feel lost, take a deep breath and remember: every great transformation starts with the first step. Shall we go together?

Chapter 1: Preparing the Ground – The Basics You Need to Know

Before starting any work (and trust me, the temptation to jump into action is great!), it's essential to understand some basic concepts:

What characterizes a small renovation? It's one that doesn't involve structural changes, such as tearing down walls or altering plumbing and electrical installations. It usually includes replacing finishes, painting, and swapping cabinets or countertops.

Budget: The most frequent question: "How much will I spend?". Costs can vary significantly. A small renovation can cost between R$5,000 and R$20,000, depending on the materials and services chosen. Later, we'll present economical alternatives.

Time: On average, a small renovation takes 7 to 15 days. Since the kitchen is the heart of the home, plan how you'll organize yourself during this period. Practical tip: set up a temporary kitchen in another space, like a balcony or laundry area.

Legal aspects: According to the CAU-BR, renovations that don't alter the property's structure generally don't require approved projects. However, always consult the specific norms of your municipality.

Chapter 2: Step by Step – Let's Get to Work!

  1. Identify the problems: Make a detailed list of everything that needs improvement in your kitchen. Worn-out cabinets? Damaged flooring? Insufficient lighting? This analysis will be your guide.
  2. Set priorities: With a limited budget, focus on the changes that will have the greatest impact. A new paint job and modern handles can transform the space with a relatively low investment.
  3. Spatial planning: Take precise measurements and make a sketch. Remember the concept of the "work triangle": refrigerator, sink, and stove should form a functional triangle.
    Pro tip: Keep at least 1.20m between the elements of the triangle to ensure comfortable circulation.
  4. Material selection: For countertops, granite remains an excellent option, but there are more economical alternatives like marble. For flooring, porcelain offers durability and ease of cleaning.
  5. Decide whether to hire or DIY: Painting and hardware replacement are tasks you can do yourself. However, installing new flooring or cabinets usually requires qualified professionals. For tips on choosing a good mason, consult our complete guide.
  6. Demolition phase: Start by removing all elements that will be replaced. Protect what will remain with plastic and cardboard. Pay special attention to plumbing and wiring!
  7. Correct execution sequence: First the floor (if it's being replaced), then walls, cabinets, and finally the finishes. Think of the process as dressing an onion – from the inside out.
  8. Final touches: This is the special touch! New handles, adequate LED lighting, a beautiful backsplash – small details that make a big difference.

Chapter 3: Advanced Tips – For Those Who Want to Stand Out

  • Layered lighting: Combine general (ceiling), functional (under cabinets), and decorative (pendant) lighting. This combination creates a pleasant atmosphere and functionality.
  • Cabinets up to the ceiling: Besides the modern look, they prevent dust accumulation on top of traditional cabinets.
  • Backsplash as a highlight: A unique finish or even washable paint can be the surprise element of your kitchen.
  • Smart organization solutions: Drawer organizers, adjustable shelves, and spice niches optimize every available inch.
  • Sensor faucet: A slightly higher investment, but it offers practicality and hygiene, especially when hands are busy with food preparation.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

  1. Not testing the layout: Use masking tape on the floor to simulate the arrangement of new furniture before buying it. This way, you'll check if there's enough space to open doors and drawers.
  2. Prioritizing only aesthetics: That beautiful but slippery floor near the wet area? Bad choice. Always consider the functionality of materials.
  3. Saving on the wrong professionals: A good mason is worth the investment. Poorly planned savings can result in costly rework.
  4. Neglecting ventilation: A kitchen without a proper exhaust fan accumulates grease and odors. If installing one isn't possible, ensure at least a well-positioned window.
  5. Ignoring ergonomics: Countertops that are too low or high cause discomfort. The standard is 90cm, but adjust according to your height.

FAQ – Frequently Asked Questions

Can I renovate my kitchen in stages?

Yes, but with planning! Start with the structural elements that won't be modified later. For example: first flooring and walls, then cabinets, and finally the details. Dividing into phases is feasible but can increase costs due to multiple material shipments.

Is it worth renovating cabinets instead of replacing them?

Certainly! Proper sanding and specific furniture paint can revitalize old cabinets with reduced investment. This solution isn't recommended if the structure is compromised or the layout is inadequate. Check out our complete guide on cabinet painting.

What's the best color palette for a small kitchen?

Light tones create a sense of spaciousness, but a pop of color on specific elements (like lower cabinets or the back wall) adds personality. White, light gray, and soft blues are excellent options.

Is hiring an architect necessary for a small renovation?

According to the CAU-BR, for simple alterations it's not mandatory, but a professional can prevent mistakes and optimize spaces. For changes in sink or stove position, specialized consultation is recommended.

Conclusion: Ready to Start?

We've reached the end of the guide, but this is just the beginning of your transformation journey. Remember: every significant change starts with a first step. How about starting today by making your priority list?
